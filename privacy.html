Privacy Policy for Blinkam

Last updated: 14 November 2026

Blinkam is an app that warns drivers about speed cameras. This page explains what information the app uses, where it goes, and what you can do about it.

Short version

  • Blinkam uses your phone's location while the app is open, to show where you are and to warn you about cameras ahead.
  • Your location, your camera reports and your settings stay on your phone. Blinkam has no server, so we never receive them.
  • Maps and place search come from Google (Android) or Apple (iPhone). They receive what they need to show the map and find places.
  • If you turn on the simulated drive (a demo), your last position and the demo cameras on the way go to an OpenStreetMap route server to find real roads.
  • To get app updates, newer versions of Blinkam ask Expo's update server for new app code. This sends a random install ID, never your location or reports.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking. We never sell data.

What the app uses and why

Location

If you allow it, Blinkam reads your location (precise, if you allow precise location) while the app is open. It uses it to show your position and speed on the map, to find speed cameras near you and warn you before you reach them, and to fill in the position when you report a camera. Blinkam does not use your location in the background. Your current location is only kept in the phone's memory while the app runs. It is not saved permanently and not sent to us.

Camera reports and ratings

When you report or rate a camera, Blinkam saves the camera's position, type, direction, speed limit and the time on your phone. In this version, reports and ratings are not shared with other drivers and are not sent to us.

App ID

The first time you open Blinkam, it creates a random ID on your phone. It is only used to tell your own reports and ratings apart from others. It is not linked to your name, phone number or advertising ID, and it stays on your phone.

Settings

Your choices (such as units, map style and warning sounds) are saved on your phone.

Services from other companies

  • Maps: on Android the map comes from Google Maps, on iPhone from Apple Maps. To load the map, your phone sends requests to Google or Apple. They receive technical data such as your IP address and the map area you look at. Their privacy policies apply: Google, Apple.
  • Place search and area names: when you search for a place, or when the app shows the name of the area you are in, the text you type or the map position is sent to your phone's built-in place lookup service (Google on Android, Apple on iPhone).
  • Roads for the simulated drive: only when you turn on Home → Settings → Demo → Simulated drive, Blinkam sends your last known position and the positions of the demo cameras on the way to the free OpenStreetMap route server of FOSSGIS e.V. (Germany), to find a route along real roads. Like any internet request, this includes your IP address. FOSSGIS saves route requests in its server log: FOSSGIS privacy policy (German).
  • App updates: versions of Blinkam released after 14 November 2026 ask the update server of Expo (650 Industries, Inc., USA) each time they start whether newer app code is available, and download it if so. This request contains a random install ID that is only used for updates, the app version, your phone's operating system and, like any internet request, your IP address. If an update failed to start, it also contains a short error message. It never contains your location, reports or settings. Expo uses this to deliver updates and to count how many installs received them: Expo privacy policy.
  • App stores: Google Play and the App Store may collect technical information, such as crash reports, under their own privacy policies.

What we don't do

Blinkam has no user accounts, shows no ads, contains no analytics or tracking tools, and does not sell or share personal data.

How long data is kept and how to delete it

Everything Blinkam saves stays on your phone until you delete it. To delete your reports and ratings, open Home → Settings → Reset demo data. Uninstalling the app deletes everything. You can turn off location access at any time in your phone's settings; the map and warnings then can't follow your position.

Your rights

Under data protection law, such as the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), you have the right to ask about, correct or delete personal data about you, to restrict or object to its use, and to withdraw your consent. Because Blinkam keeps your data on your phone, you can do most of this yourself as described above. For anything else, write to blinkam.team@gmail.com. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority.

Children

Blinkam is made for drivers and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

We will update this page before Blinkam adds features that send data to a server (for example sharing camera reports with other drivers) or shows ads. The date at the top shows the latest change.
